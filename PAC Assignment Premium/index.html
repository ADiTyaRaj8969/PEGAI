Prompt Engineering Techniques

Each technique is applied when generating lecture notes. Learn how they work.

🎭

Role Prompting

The AI is assigned a specific persona such as “You are an expert professor of Computer Science with 20 years of teaching experience.” This contextualises the response style, depth, and tone for academic content.

Example: “As a senior faculty member, explain the A* algorithm to 3rd-year engineering students...”
📐

Structured Prompting

Prompts include explicit headings, numbered sections, and constraints so the AI produces output in a predictable, well-organised format matching academic standards.

Example: “Generate notes with sections: 1. Objective 2. Theory 3. Algorithm 4. Example...”
📋

Template Prompting

A pre-defined academic template with placeholder variables is populated by the AI. Ensures consistency across all lecture notes in a course or department.

Example: “Fill this template: TOPIC: [X], LEARNING_OBJ: [Y], CONTENT: [fill]...”
📚

Few-Shot Prompting

A few high-quality examples of lecture note sections are provided to the AI before the actual request, guiding the model to match the expected format, detail, and style.

Example: “Here are two sample sections [examples]. Now generate a similar section for [topic]...”
🔗

Chain Prompting

A complex lecture note is generated in multiple sequential steps. First generate the outline, then expand each section, then add examples, then add questions. Each step's output feeds the next.

Example: Step 1: Outline → Step 2: Theory → Step 3: Examples → Step 4: Questions
🔄

Iterative Refinement

The first generation serves as a draft. Feedback is provided and the AI iteratively improves the notes — increasing accuracy, depth, and pedagogical effectiveness with each pass.

Example: Draft → Review → “Add more examples” → Revised → Final

Quality

Content Evaluation Framework

Evaluate AI-generated lecture notes across multiple quality dimensions.

Evaluation Criteria

✅Technical Accuracy

Correctness of concepts, formulas, and algorithms per standard textbooks.

📚Curriculum Alignment

Content maps to Bloom's taxonomy level and course outcomes specified.

🎯Pedagogical Effectiveness

Notes facilitate meaningful student learning and comprehension.

📊Completeness

All required sections are present with sufficient detail.

🔄Consistency

Uniform terminology, notation, and writing style throughout.

💡Readability

Content is clear, concise, and appropriate for the target audience.

⚠Hallucination Risk

AI-generated facts must be cross-verified with standard references.

🔒Ethical AI Usage

Content respects copyright, avoids plagiarism, and discloses AI assistance.

🛡 Responsible AI Strategies

  • 01
    Always validate AI-generated content against standard textbooks and official documentation before classroom use.
  • 02
    Use iterative refinement — never accept the first draft. Provide specific feedback to improve accuracy and depth.
  • 03
    Disclose AI assistance to students and maintain transparency about the role of Generative AI in content creation.
  • 04
    Protect data privacy — do not include personally identifiable information in prompts sent to external AI APIs.
  • 05
    Cross-reference citations — AI may fabricate citations. Always verify that papers and books cited actually exist.
  • 06
    Maintain academic standards — AI content should complement, not replace, a faculty member's domain expertise and judgment.
